Several IoT-based platforms for indoor environmental monitoring have
been proposed in recent years. This section discusses two
representative academic systems --- the on-premises monitoring and
prediction platform by Mumtaz et al.~\cite{mumtaz2021internet} and the
iAQ+ laboratory supervision system by Marques and
Pitarma~\cite{marques2019internet} --- and contrasts their backend
architecture, sampling strategy, and notification design with the
design decisions taken for Sensiq.

\subsection{On-Premises IoT Air Quality Monitoring with Predictive Analytics}\label{sec:rw_mumtaz}

Mumtaz et al.~\cite{mumtaz2021internet} present an IoT-based indoor
air quality monitoring and prediction platform motivated by the
COVID-19 pandemic. Their sensor node combines an ATmega328P
microcontroller with a NodeMCU Wi\=/Fi module and a broad set of
sensors covering eight pollutants
(NH\textsubscript{3}, CO, NO\textsubscript{2}, CH\textsubscript{4},
CO\textsubscript{2}, PM\,2.5) as well as ambient temperature and
humidity. The node, deployed in a research laboratory, transmits
readings via GSM or Wi\=/Fi to a web portal and mobile app, and
generates alerts when sensor values exceed predefined thresholds.
Beyond pure monitoring, the authors additionally apply machine
learning to the recorded data to predict future pollutant
concentrations.

The work shares Sensiq's general topology of a multi-sensor edge node,
a cloud-side analytics layer, and a web-based user interface, and the
ATmega328P with NodeMCU is functionally comparable to the ESP32 used
in Sensiq (\cref{sec:hardware}). Beyond this surface similarity,
however, the two systems pursue fundamentally different goals, and it
is precisely these differences that motivate Sensiq.

First, Mumtaz et al. rely on a local web server and database. This
ties the platform to continuous on-premises operation, complicates
remote access, and makes horizontal scaling to additional sites or
users an explicit infrastructure project. Sensiq instead targets a
fully serverless cloud backend (\cref{sec:aws_services}), which scales
on demand, removes the need to operate dedicated server hardware, and
keeps idle cost close to zero --- a key requirement for routine use in
academic and small industrial laboratories.

Second, their sampling interval of five minutes is geared towards
trend analysis and pollutant forecasting rather than immediate
reaction. For safety-relevant events such as fire exposure or sudden
gas concentration spikes, this granularity is too coarse. It is
likewise insufficient for product quality assurance, where
maintaining steady environmental parameters such as temperature and
humidity is essential and short-lived deviations must be detected
before they compromise samples or production batches. Sensiq's
MQTT-over-TLS pipeline is designed for low-latency live monitoring and
threshold-driven email alerts, so that critical conditions surface
within seconds rather than minutes.

Third, the reported battery autonomy of around twenty hours limits
the node to scenarios with permanent power or frequent recharging.
While both the ATmega328P/NodeMCU combination and the ESP32 in
principle support deep-sleep operation, Mumtaz et al. do not exploit
it. Sensiq does not yet implement deep sleep either, but its modular
ESP32-based node is explicitly prepared for this extension, which is
identified as future work in \cref{sec:summary} to enable
substantially longer unattended operation.

In summary, Mumtaz et al.~\cite{mumtaz2021internet}
demonstrate that a multi-sensor IoT node
combined with cloud-side analytics is a viable approach to indoor
environmental monitoring, and their predictive layer illustrates how
such a platform can be enriched with forecasting. Sensiq adopts the
same overall topology but deliberately shifts the emphasis: away from
an on-premises stack with coarse, forecast-oriented sampling, towards
a serverless backend with low-latency live monitoring, threshold
alerts, and durable historical storage accessible via open REST
endpoints.

\subsection{iAQ+ Laboratory Environment Supervision System}\label{sec:rw_marques}
Marques and Pitarma~\cite{marques2019internet} present iAQ+, an IoT
based environmental quality management system for laboratory
conditions. An ESP8266 node equipped with a Bosch BME680 sensor
captures temperature, relative humidity, barometric pressure, and a
qualitative air quality index, and forwards them to a web management
system and an iOS smartphone application that supports configurable
thresholds and push notifications. The sensor selection and the
Wi\=/Fi-based node topology resemble Sensiq's hardware
(\cref{sec:hardware}), but the differences in backend, frontend, and
timing behaviour are what matter for Sensiq's positioning.

On the backend side, iAQ+ is built on a SQL Server database accessed
through the .NET framework, i.e. a classical server-based stack with
the same operational drawbacks already discussed for Mumtaz et al.:
the platform requires a continuously running server, and scaling to
additional sites or tenants is an explicit infrastructure activity.
Sensiq instead delegates persistence and request handling to managed
serverless AWS services (\cref{sec:aws_services}), so that scaling and
availability are handled by the cloud provider and idle cost remains
negligible.

The frontend design of iAQ+ is also more restrictive than Sensiq's.
The smartphone application is limited to iOS\,12 and above, with no
Android counterpart, which excludes a significant portion of potential
users. The accompanying web dashboard exposes historical data only at
hourly, daily, and monthly aggregations and does not provide a true
live view or short-term (e.g. minute-level) history. Sensiq targets
platform-independent access through a React-based web frontend served
over open REST endpoints and explicitly distinguishes a low-latency
live route from a long-term history route
(\cref{sec:user_interaction}).

Finally, iAQ+ does not state a measurement and transmission frequency,
nor does it quantify the latency between a threshold violation and the
delivered push notification. For safety-relevant events such as fire
exposure or sudden gas concentration spikes, this lack of guaranteed
timing is a substantive limitation. Sensiq is designed around an
explicit low-latency MQTT-over-TLS pipeline and threshold-driven email
alerts so that critical conditions surface within seconds, and its
notification path is part of the evaluation in \cref{sec:evaluation}.
